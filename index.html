Why the same machines carry more work every year

The machines you reserve in year one do not get slower. Everything that runs on them gets faster, and it gets faster quickly.

Better open models keep arriving. Model builders have found that a large model does not need all of itself switched on to answer a question. Qwen3 Coder Next scores 74.2% on a public software benchmark while using only three billion of its parameters for any given word. A model built that way answers as well as one many times its size and costs a fraction as much to run. New ones land every few months, and each is a straight upgrade on hardware you already own.

The software that serves them keeps improving. The most useful trick in production today has a small, fast model draft the next few words and the large model check the whole draft in one pass. Most drafts are correct, so the expensive model runs far less often. Writing answers is where roughly 60% of the work goes, so this alone lifts throughput meaningfully. Models are also being run on smaller number formats, which fits more of them onto the same chip without a noticeable loss of quality.

Your own specialist models compound. Every month of production traffic shows us patterns the month before did not. Each pattern that repeats often enough becomes another small trained model that handles that work at a fraction of the tokens. Year two finds work year one could not see, so the share of your workload running on cheap specialists grows on its own.

Why owning the reserve is what lets you keep the gains

On a metered service, every one of those improvements lands on the provider's side of the meter. They decide whether any of it reaches your invoice, and when. You pay per token either way, so a model that becomes twice as efficient to run does not make your bill smaller unless someone chooses to reprice it.

When the capacity is reserved for you and the specialist models are trained on your own work, the arithmetic runs the other way. Efficiency arrives as extra headroom on machines you have already paid for. You adopt frontier capability on performance, accuracy and cost as it ships, rather than waiting for it to be passed down to you. The same GPUs spread across more work each year, so their cost amortizes over a growing base instead of resetting every billing cycle.

That headroom is yours to spend however the business needs it. You can grow into it and hold your budget flat. You can trade it for better answers by running more attempts per task. You can trade it for speed by serving with more of the model resident. Or you can put it into training more specialists, which frees more headroom again.
